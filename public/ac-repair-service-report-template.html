Quick Answer

What should an AC repair service report include?

An AC repair service report should explain the customer’s reported issue, what the technician found, what work was completed, and what follow-up items should be reviewed with the customer.

The best reports are clear, factual, and easy for the customer and office staff to understand later. Do not invent readings, parts, prices, warranty terms, safety claims, or final system condition unless those details are confirmed in the technician notes.

What to Include

What to include in an AC repair service report

  • Customer name or job reference
  • Service date
  • Reported issue, such as no cooling, weak airflow, or system not turning on
  • Equipment type, if known
  • Technician findings
  • Parts replaced, if applicable
  • System checks performed
  • Filter condition
  • Coil condition
  • Drain line or condensate notes, if applicable
  • Temperature split or operating notes, if documented
  • Completed work
  • Recommended next steps
  • Customer declined items, if applicable
  • Review notes for anything that needs confirmation

Example

Example AC repair service report

Here is a simple example based on rough technician notes. 

AC Repair Service Report

Customer / Job:
Residential AC repair visit

Service Date:
[Add date]

Reported Issue:
Customer reported that the AC system was running but not cooling properly.

Equipment / System:
Central air conditioning system

Technician Findings:
The technician found a weak capacitor and a clogged filter. The outdoor coil was dirty. The system was cooling during the visit with an 18-degree temperature split.

Work Completed:
The weak capacitor was replaced. The technician checked system operation and documented the filter and coil condition.

Parts / Materials:
- Capacitor replacement, if confirmed by invoice or technician notes

System Notes:
- Filter was clogged
- Outdoor coil was dirty
- System was cooling during visit
- Temperature split documented as 18 degrees

Recommendations:
Replace the clogged filter and consider outdoor coil cleaning. Routine maintenance may also be worth discussing if the customer wants regular system checkups.

Customer Notes:
Customer declined coil cleaning today.

Review Notes:
Review before sending. Confirm all parts, pricing, warranty language, safety-related wording, and recommendations.

This type of visit can also support short invoice wording, a customer follow-up, or estimate wording if the customer needs recommended work priced out.

Invoice Wording

AC repair invoice wording example

Diagnosed AC cooling concern, replaced confirmed weak capacitor, checked system operation, and documented filter and outdoor coil condition.

Invoice wording should usually be shorter than the full AC repair report. Confirm pricing, labor, parts, taxes, discounts, warranty language, and invoice totals before sending.

Customer Follow-Up

AC repair customer follow-up example

Thank you for having us out today. We checked the AC cooling concern, completed the documented repair, and reviewed system operation after service. Please review the recommendations in the service report and contact us if you would like help scheduling the follow-up items.

Follow-up messages should be short, professional, and based only on confirmed AC repair notes.

Estimate Wording

AC repair estimate wording example

Recommend replacing the clogged filter and cleaning the outdoor condenser coil to address documented airflow and coil condition concerns noted during the AC repair visit.

Estimate wording should explain the recommended work without exaggerating urgency or guaranteeing future performance. Review recommended work, pricing, options, warranty language, exclusions, and customer approval requirements before sending.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who is this AC repair service report template for?

It is for HVAC/R owners, office managers, technicians, and admin staff who need a simple way to document AC repair visits and customer-facing cooling service notes.

Can I copy this into my service software?

Yes, you can use it as a starting point. Review and adjust the wording before adding it to Jobber, QuickBooks, another office system, or a customer-facing report.

What kind of AC repair notes should I include?

Include the reported issue, technician findings, completed work, parts used, filter condition, coil condition, drain notes, temperature split, customer declined items, and recommended next steps when those details are available.

Should this template include prices?

Only include pricing if it has been verified by your company. This template does not create prices, discounts, taxes, labor rates, warranty terms, or final totals.

Can this say the AC system is fixed?

Only say the system is fixed if the technician notes clearly support that. Safer wording is to describe what was found, what was completed, and what was observed during the visit.

Can this template help with estimate wording?

Yes. Use the report to document confirmed findings, then use estimate wording only for recommended work that still needs pricing, scope review, and customer approval.

Should I use the AI tool instead of the template?

Use the template when you want a manual structure. Use the HVAC Service Report Generator when you have rough AC repair notes and want help turning them into a cleaner report, invoice description, follow-up message, and internal summary.

What should I review before sending an AC repair report?

Review parts, pricing, temperature readings, completed work, recommendations, warranty language, declined work, estimate scope, and any safety-related wording before sending.
