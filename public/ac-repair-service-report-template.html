AC Repair Service Report Template

Use this template to document AC repair visits more clearly.

This page gives HVAC/R owners, office staff, and technicians a simple structure for turning AC repair notes into a cleaner customer-ready report.

Quick answer

A good AC repair service report should explain the customer’s reported issue, what the technician checked, what was found, what work was completed, and what follow-up action is recommended.

Keep the report clear, accurate, and easy for the customer to understand. Do not add parts, pricing, warranty terms, test results, or safety claims unless they are confirmed.

What to include in an AC repair service report

  • Customer name or job reference
  • Service date
  • Reported issue, such as no cooling, weak airflow, or system not turning on
  • Equipment type, if known
  • Technician findings
  • Parts replaced, if applicable
  • System checks performed
  • Filter condition
  • Coil condition
  • Drain line or condensate notes, if applicable
  • Temperature split or operating notes, if documented
  • Completed work
  • Recommended next steps
  • Customer declined items, if applicable
  • Review notes for anything that needs confirmation

Simple AC repair service report template

Copy this structure and adjust it to match the technician’s actual notes. 

AC Repair Service Report

Customer / Job:
[Customer name or job reference]

Service Date:
[Date]

Reported Issue:
[No cooling, weak airflow, system not turning on, thermostat issue, etc.]

Equipment / System:
[AC system, heat pump, rooftop unit, etc.]

Technician Findings:
[Summarize what the technician found in plain English.]

Work Completed:
[Summarize the work completed during the visit.]

Parts / Materials:
[List only confirmed parts or materials used.]

System Notes:
[Add temperature split, filter condition, coil condition, drain notes, or operating notes if documented.]

Recommendations:
[List recommended cleaning, repairs, maintenance, replacement, or follow-up items.]

Customer Notes:
[Include declined work, requested follow-up, or special notes if applicable.]

Review Notes:
Review all details before sending to the customer. Confirm parts, pricing, warranty language, safety claims, measurements, and final recommendations.

Example AC repair service report

Here is a simple example based on rough technician notes. 

AC Repair Service Report

Customer / Job:
Residential AC repair visit

Service Date:
[Add date]

Reported Issue:
Customer reported that the AC system was running but not cooling properly.

Equipment / System:
Central air conditioning system

Technician Findings:
The technician found a weak capacitor and a clogged filter. The outdoor coil was dirty. The system was cooling during the visit with an 18-degree temperature split.

Work Completed:
The weak capacitor was replaced. The technician checked system operation and documented the filter and coil condition.

Parts / Materials:
- Capacitor replacement, if confirmed by invoice or technician notes

System Notes:
- Filter was clogged
- Outdoor coil was dirty
- System was cooling during visit
- Temperature split documented as 18 degrees

Recommendations:
Replace the clogged filter and consider outdoor coil cleaning. Routine maintenance may also be worth discussing if the customer wants regular system checkups.

Customer Notes:
Customer declined coil cleaning today.

Review Notes:
Review before sending. Confirm all parts, pricing, warranty language, safety-related wording, and recommendations.

Frequently asked questions

Who is this AC repair service report template for?

It is for HVAC/R owners, office managers, technicians, and admin staff who need a simple way to document AC repair visits and customer-facing service notes.

Can I copy this into my service software?

Yes, you can use it as a starting point. Review and adjust the wording before adding it to Jobber, QuickBooks, another office system, or a customer-facing report.

Should this template include prices?

Only include pricing if it has been verified by your company. This template does not create prices, discounts, taxes, labor rates, warranty terms, or final totals.

Can this say the AC system is fixed?

Only say the system is fixed if the technician notes clearly support that. Safer wording is to describe what was found, what was completed, and what was observed during the visit.
