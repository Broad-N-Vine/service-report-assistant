Quick Answer

How should you write a walk-in cooler service report?

A walk-in cooler service report should explain the customer’s reported issue, what the technician checked, what was found, what work was completed, and what follow-up items should be reviewed with the customer.

The report should stay factual. Do not invent box temperatures, refrigerant readings, pressure readings, electrical readings, food safety claims, warranty terms, or final equipment condition unless those details are confirmed in the technician notes.

How to use it

Write a better walk-in cooler report in three steps

1. Start with the complaint

Include what the customer reported, such as the cooler running warm, not holding temperature, icing, unusual noise, water near the unit, or product concerns.

2. Describe confirmed findings

Include only what the technician actually found, cleaned, repaired, replaced, checked, or recommended during the refrigeration service visit.

3. Review before sending

Confirm readings, pricing, warranty language, food safety wording, customer approval, recommendations, and final operating notes before sending.

Technician Notes

Example rough technician notes

Customer reported walk-in cooler not holding temperature. Found condenser coil dirty and restricted with debris. Cleaned condenser coil and cleared debris from unit area. Checked general operation after cleaning. Recommended monitoring cooler temperature and scheduling follow-up if temperature concerns continue.

These notes include the customer complaint, technician finding, completed work, post-service check, and recommendation.

Example Report

Walk-in cooler service report example

Commercial Refrigeration Service Report

Customer / Job:
Commercial walk-in cooler service call

Service Date:
[Date]

Reason for Visit:
Customer reported the walk-in cooler was not holding temperature.

Equipment / System:
Walk-in cooler refrigeration system.

Technician Findings:
Technician found the condenser coil dirty and restricted with debris.

Work Completed:
Cleaned the condenser coil and cleared debris from the unit area. Checked general operation after cleaning.

Parts / Materials:
No replacement parts documented in the technician notes.

System Notes:
General operation was checked after cleaning. Any box temperature readings, pressure readings, electrical readings, or final operating conditions should be confirmed before sending.

Recommendations:
Customer should monitor cooler temperature and schedule follow-up service if temperature concerns continue.

Customer Notes:
No customer-specific notes documented.

Review Notes:
Confirm pricing, temperature readings, final operating condition, warranty language, food safety wording, and recommendations before sending to the customer.
Review before sending: Confirm customer details, cooler condition, temperature readings, completed work, pricing, warranty language, food safety wording, and final recommendations before using this report.
Before and After

Vague vs. better walk-in cooler report wording

Too vague

Cooler was warm. Cleaned unit.

This does not explain what was found, what was cleaned, or what the customer should monitor.

Better

Customer reported walk-in cooler not holding temperature. Found condenser coil dirty and restricted with debris. Cleaned condenser coil, cleared debris from unit area, and checked general operation after cleaning.

This gives the customer and office staff a clearer record of the issue and completed work.

Invoice Wording

Walk-in cooler invoice wording example

Cleaned dirty walk-in cooler condenser coil, cleared debris from unit area, and checked general operation after cleaning.

Invoice wording should usually be shorter than the full service report. Confirm pricing, labor, taxes, discounts, warranty language, and invoice totals before sending.

Customer Follow-Up

Walk-in cooler customer follow-up example

Thank you for having us out today. We checked the walk-in cooler for the reported temperature concern, cleaned the dirty condenser coil, and checked general operation after service. Please monitor the cooler temperature and contact us if temperature concerns continue.

Follow-up messages should be short, professional, and based only on confirmed service notes.

Common Mistakes

Common walk-in cooler service report mistakes to avoid

  • Being too vague: avoid notes like “cooler fixed” without explaining what was found.
  • Inventing readings: do not add box temperatures, pressure readings, or electrical readings unless documented.
  • Making food safety claims: avoid product safety or spoilage claims unless your company has documented and approved them.
  • Overpromising: do not guarantee long-term temperature control or say the system is perfect unless verified.
  • Forgetting review: pricing, warranty wording, customer approval, and final condition should be checked before sending.

FAQ

FAQ

How do you write a walk-in cooler service report?

Explain what the customer reported, what the technician checked, what was found, what work was completed, and what should be reviewed before sending the report.

Should the report include box temperature?

Only include box temperature if it was documented by the technician. Do not invent temperature readings or final operating conditions.

Should the report mention food safety?

Only mention food safety, product condition, or spoilage if your company has documented and approved that wording. Otherwise, keep the report focused on the equipment service performed.

Can I use this for invoice wording?

Yes, but invoice wording should usually be shorter than the full report. Focus on the confirmed work performed and any documented parts or cleaning.

Can AI help write commercial refrigeration service reports?

AI can help clean up rough technician notes, but the final report should be reviewed by a person before it is sent to a customer.
