Quick Answer

What should HVAC estimate wording include?

HVAC estimate wording should clearly explain the recommended work, the issue or finding that supports it, and what the customer should understand before approving the repair, replacement, maintenance item, or follow-up work.

Estimate descriptions should stay factual. Do not invent pricing, parts, labor, taxes, discounts, warranty language, safety claims, code-compliance wording, final scope, or guaranteed outcomes unless those details have been verified by the office.

Review before sending: The generator creates wording only. Final scope, pricing, parts, labor, taxes, discounts, warranty language, customer approval, and company-specific estimate rules should be reviewed by a person.

What to include

Good estimate descriptions work best when the notes explain the issue, the recommended work, why the work is being recommended, and what the customer should understand before approving the estimate.

Do not let the tool create prices, warranty terms, final scope, exclusions, or guaranteed outcomes. Those details should be verified by the office before sending.

Estimate vs. Invoice

How is estimate wording different from invoice wording?

Estimate wording explains recommended work before the customer approves it. Invoice wording explains work that has already been completed or approved for billing. Keep those two types of wording separate.

Use estimate wording for

  • Recommended repairs
  • Replacement options
  • Customer approval requests
  • Future work descriptions

Use invoice wording for

  • Completed work
  • Approved billable work
  • Confirmed parts and labor
  • Customer billing records

Save in CRM for

  • Estimate sent date
  • Customer approval status
  • Follow-up deadline
  • Revision or decline notes
Common Mistakes

Common HVAC estimate wording mistakes to avoid

  • Writing the estimate like completed work: estimate wording should describe recommended or proposed work, not work that has already been completed.
  • Inventing pricing: do not allow AI to create prices, taxes, labor rates, discounts, deposits, balances, or final totals.
  • Overstating urgency: avoid pressure-heavy language unless urgency is documented and company-approved.
  • Adding unsupported warranty terms: only mention warranty details that your company has confirmed.
  • Skipping approval tracking: save whether the customer approved, declined, requested a revision, or needs follow-up.
  • Leaving scope unclear: make sure the proposed work is specific enough for the customer and office to understand.
