Quick Answer

What should an HVAC maintenance agreement follow-up include?

An HVAC maintenance agreement follow-up should thank the customer, briefly connect the message to the recent service visit, explain that routine maintenance can help keep future service organized, and offer a simple next step for learning about available maintenance options.

The message should stay factual and low-pressure. Do not invent discounts, priority scheduling, included services, emergency coverage, warranty benefits, repair guarantees, cancellation rules, or plan terms unless those details are confirmed by the company.

Best practice: Frame the agreement as an option the customer can review. Do not make the customer feel pressured, and do not make claims about savings, performance, fewer repairs, or coverage unless those claims are approved.

How to Use It

Write a better maintenance agreement follow-up in three steps

1. Start with the service visit

Mention the recent tune-up, repair, inspection, filter replacement, no-cooling call, no-heat call, or other completed visit when appropriate.

2. Keep the offer helpful

Position the maintenance agreement as an option the customer can review, not as a pressure-heavy sales message.

3. Review the plan details

Confirm plan name, pricing, included visits, discounts, scheduling details, exclusions, cancellation rules, and approved company wording before sending.

Before and After

Pushy vs. better maintenance agreement wording

Too pushy

You need to sign up for our maintenance plan now to avoid expensive repairs.

This sounds overly aggressive and may make unsupported promises about future repairs.

Better

Our technician recommended continuing routine maintenance. If you would like to stay on a regular service schedule, our office can share the current maintenance agreement options for review.

This is softer, more factual, and gives the customer a clear next step without overpromising.

Email vs. Text

Should maintenance agreement follow-up be an email or text?

Email is usually better when the customer needs plan details, a longer explanation, or approved company wording. Text can work when the message is only a quick invitation to review options.

Use email for

  • Plan details
  • Longer explanations
  • Customer-requested information
  • Approved company wording

Use text for

  • Simple follow-up reminders
  • Short customer check-ins
  • Inviting the customer to review options
  • Asking whether they want details

Office Review

Maintenance agreement wording checklist

  • Confirm the customer name and recent service visit.
  • Confirm the maintenance recommendation is supported by the notes.
  • Confirm the customer showed interest, or keep the message especially soft.
  • Confirm the plan name before using it.
  • Confirm pricing before mentioning it.
  • Confirm included visits or services before listing them.
  • Confirm discounts or priority scheduling before mentioning them.
  • Confirm exclusions, limitations, cancellation rules, and payment terms when needed.
  • Avoid repair guarantees, performance promises, or savings claims unless approved.
Common Mistakes

Common maintenance agreement follow-up mistakes to avoid

  • Being too pushy: avoid pressure-heavy wording that makes the customer feel forced.
  • Inventing benefits: do not add discounts, priority scheduling, or included visits unless confirmed.
  • Overpromising: do not guarantee fewer repairs, lower bills, better comfort, or longer equipment life.
  • Using vague plan terms: confirm the actual maintenance agreement details before sending.
  • Skipping review: pricing, plan details, company wording, and customer-specific notes should be checked before sending.
  • Missing the follow-up task: save whether the customer wants details, declined the offer, or should be contacted later.

FAQ

What should I say when following up about an HVAC maintenance agreement?

Thank the customer, connect the message to the recent service visit, mention the routine maintenance recommendation, and offer to send the current maintenance agreement details.

Should I include pricing in the follow-up?

Only include pricing if it has been confirmed by your company. Otherwise, offer to send the current details or ask the customer to contact the office.

Should I mention discounts or priority scheduling?

Only mention discounts, priority scheduling, included visits, or special terms if those benefits are part of the current approved maintenance agreement.

Can this be used as a text message?

Yes. Use one of the shorter versions for text messages and keep the wording clear, friendly, and factual.

Should maintenance agreement follow-ups be saved in the customer record?

Yes. Save the final message, customer response, interest level, requested plan details, and any future follow-up task in the customer record.

Can AI help write maintenance agreement follow-ups?

AI can help turn rough notes into a customer-ready maintenance agreement message, but the final wording should be reviewed by a person before it is sent.
