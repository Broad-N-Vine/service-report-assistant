Quick Answer

What should an HVAC tune-up report include?

A good HVAC tune-up report should summarize the service visit, list the basic checks performed, document what the technician found, explain any completed work, and clearly note recommended next steps.

The goal is not to overcomplicate the paperwork. The goal is to make the tune-up easy for the customer, office staff, and technician to understand later.

Example

Example tune-up report

Here is a simple example based on rough technician notes. 

HVAC Tune-Up Report

Customer / Job:
Residential AC maintenance visit

Service Date:
[Add date]

Equipment / System:
Central air conditioning system

Reason for Visit:
Seasonal AC maintenance tune-up

Work Performed:
The technician completed a maintenance visit and checked general system operation. The filter condition was reviewed, the outdoor coil was inspected, and system performance was documented during the visit.

Technician Findings:
The filter was clogged and the outdoor coil was dirty. The system was cooling during the visit with an 18-degree temperature split.

Completed Work:
The technician checked system operation and documented the condition of the filter and outdoor coil.

Recommendations:
Replace the clogged filter and consider outdoor coil cleaning. Routine maintenance may help keep service reminders organized and make future system checks easier to track.

Customer Notes:
Customer declined coil cleaning today.

Review Notes:
Review this report before sending. Confirm all service details, recommendations, pricing, warranty language, and safety-related wording.

Frequently asked questions

Who is this HVAC tune-up report template for?

It is for HVAC/R owners, office managers, technicians, and admin staff who need a simple way to document maintenance visits and customer-facing tune-up notes.

Can I copy this template into my service software?

Yes, you can use it as a starting point. Review and adjust the wording before adding it to Jobber, QuickBooks, another office system, or a customer-facing report.

Should this template include pricing?

Only include pricing if it has been verified by your company. This template does not create prices, labor rates, part costs, discounts, taxes, warranty terms, or final totals.

Can I use this as the final customer report?

Use it as a starting point. Confirm all technical details, measurements, completed work, parts, recommendations, safety-related wording, and customer-specific notes before sending.

Can this template say the system is safe or operating perfectly?

Only use safety, performance, or final operating claims if the technician notes clearly support that wording and your company policy allows it. Safer wording is to describe what was checked and documented.

Should I use the AI tool instead of the template?

Use the template when you want a simple structure to copy and fill in manually. Use the HVAC Service Report Generator when you already have rough technician notes and want help turning them into cleaner wording.

What should I review before sending a tune-up report?

Review measurements, filter condition, coil condition, completed work, recommendations, declined work, pricing, warranty language, and any safety-related statements before sending.
