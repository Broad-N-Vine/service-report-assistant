Quick Answer

What should a furnace repair service report include?

A furnace repair service report should explain the customer’s reported heating issue, what the technician found, what work was completed, and what follow-up items should be reviewed with the customer.

The best reports are clear, factual, and easy for the customer and office staff to understand later.

What to include in a furnace repair service report

  • Customer name or job reference
  • Service date
  • Reported issue, such as no heat, short cycling, unusual noise, or ignition problem
  • Equipment type, if known
  • Technician findings
  • Parts replaced, if applicable
  • System checks performed
  • Filter condition
  • Ignition or flame sensor notes, if documented
  • Blower operation notes, if documented
  • Thermostat or control notes, if documented
  • Completed work
  • Recommended next steps
  • Customer declined items, if applicable
  • Review notes for anything that needs confirmation

Example

Example furnace repair service report

Here is a simple example based on rough technician notes. 

Furnace Repair Service Report

Customer / Job:
Residential furnace repair visit

Service Date:
[Add date]

Reported Issue:
Customer reported that the furnace was not heating consistently.

Equipment / System:
Gas furnace

Technician Findings:
The technician found a dirty flame sensor and a clogged air filter. The furnace was cycling during the visit. The technician noted that the system should be reviewed again if the issue continues.

Work Completed:
The flame sensor was cleaned and the furnace operation was checked during the visit. The filter condition was documented.

Parts / Materials:
No confirmed replacement parts listed in the technician notes.

System Notes:
- Flame sensor was dirty
- Filter was clogged
- Furnace was cycling during the visit
- Further review may be needed if the issue continues

Recommendations:
Replace the clogged filter. Consider routine maintenance to help keep furnace service reminders organized and make future system checks easier to track.

Customer Notes:
Customer was advised to monitor the system and follow up if the issue continues.

Review Notes:
Review before sending. Confirm all parts, pricing, warranty language, safety-related wording, system operation notes, and recommendations.

Frequently asked questions

Who is this furnace repair service report template for?

It is for HVAC/R owners, office managers, technicians, and admin staff who need a simple way to document furnace repair visits and customer-facing heating service notes.

Can I copy this into my service software?

Yes, you can use it as a starting point. Review and adjust the wording before adding it to Jobber, QuickBooks, another office system, or a customer-facing report.

What kind of furnace repair notes should I include?

Include the reported issue, technician findings, completed work, parts used, filter condition, ignition notes, flame sensor notes, blower notes, thermostat notes, customer declined items, and recommended next steps when those details are available.

Should this template include prices?

Only include pricing if it has been verified by your company. This template does not create prices, discounts, taxes, labor rates, warranty terms, or final totals.

Can this say the furnace is safe or fully fixed?

Only use safety or final repair claims if the technician notes and your company policy clearly support that wording. Safer wording is to describe what was found, what was completed, and what should be reviewed next.
