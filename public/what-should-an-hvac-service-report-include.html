Quick Answer

What should an HVAC service report include?

An HVAC service report should include the customer or job details, reason for the visit, technician findings, work completed, parts or materials used, recommendations, customer notes, and anything that needs human review before the report is sent.

The report should be clear, factual, and based only on what actually happened during the service call. It should not invent prices, warranties, safety claims, code-compliance claims, or final operating results.

How to use it

Build a better service report in three steps

1. Start with technician notes

Use the real notes from the service call, including what the customer reported, what the technician found, and what work was completed.

2. Organize the details

Separate the notes into customer details, reported issue, findings, completed work, recommendations, and review items.

3. Review before sending

Confirm parts, pricing, measurements, warranty wording, safety language, customer-specific notes, and final recommendations before using the report.

Report Structure

Simple HVAC service report structure

A basic HVAC service report can follow this structure: 

HVAC Service Report

Customer / Job:
[Customer name or job reference]

Service Date:
[Date]

Reason for Visit:
[Customer complaint, maintenance visit, repair request, follow-up visit, etc.]

Equipment / System:
[AC system, furnace, heat pump, rooftop unit, walk-in cooler, etc.]

Technician Findings:
[Summarize what the technician found in plain English.]

Work Completed:
[Summarize confirmed work completed during the visit.]

Parts / Materials:
[List only confirmed parts or materials used.]

System Notes:
[Include operating notes, measurements, filter condition, temperature notes, or other documented details.]

Recommendations:
[List recommended repairs, maintenance, replacement, monitoring, or follow-up.]

Customer Notes:
[Include declined work, requested follow-up, access issues, scheduling notes, or special instructions.]

Review Notes:
Review all details before sending to the customer.
Review before sending: Confirm service details, parts, pricing, measurements, completed work, warranty language, safety-related wording, customer-specific notes, and final recommendations before using this report.
Checklist

What to include in an HVAC service report

  • Customer or job details: customer name, job address, unit location, or job reference.
  • Service date: the date the work was performed.
  • Reason for visit: the customer complaint, maintenance visit, repair request, or follow-up reason.
  • Equipment information: system type, unit location, model or serial numbers if actually documented.
  • Technician findings: what the technician found during inspection or diagnosis.
  • Work completed: what was actually done during the visit.
  • Parts or materials: only parts or materials that were confirmed in the notes.
  • System notes: operating notes, measurements, temperatures, filter condition, airflow notes, or other documented details.
  • Recommendations: repairs, cleaning, replacement, maintenance, monitoring, or follow-up items.
  • Customer notes: declined work, requested follow-up, access issues, scheduling details, or special instructions.
  • Review notes: anything the office or technician should confirm before sending.
Example

Example HVAC service report

HVAC Service Report

Customer / Job:
Residential service call

Service Date:
[Date]

Reason for Visit:
Customer reported weak cooling and reduced airflow from supply vents.

Equipment / System:
Residential split AC system.

Technician Findings:
Technician found a clogged air filter and a dirty outdoor condenser coil. System airflow was reduced.

Work Completed:
Replaced the air filter and cleaned the outdoor condenser coil. Checked general system operation after service.

Parts / Materials:
Replacement air filter.

System Notes:
System operation should be reviewed using the technician’s confirmed readings before sending final paperwork.

Recommendations:
Customer should continue regular filter replacement and schedule maintenance if cooling or airflow issues continue.

Customer Notes:
No customer-specific notes documented.

Review Notes:
Confirm measurements, final operating condition, pricing, and any recommendations before sending to customer.
Common Mistakes

Common HVAC service report mistakes to avoid

  • Being too vague: “fixed unit” does not explain what was found or completed.
  • Inventing details: do not add parts, readings, model numbers, or warranties that are not in the notes.
  • Making unsupported claims: avoid saying the system is safe, fully fixed, code-compliant, or under warranty unless verified.
  • Skipping recommendations: include follow-up items when the notes support them.
  • Forgetting office review: pricing, parts, warranties, and final recommendations should be checked before sending.

FAQ

FAQ

What is an HVAC service report?

An HVAC service report is a written summary of a service visit. It usually explains why the technician visited, what was found, what work was completed, and what the customer should review next.

What should a technician write in service notes?

Technician notes should include the customer complaint, equipment details, findings, completed work, parts or materials used, recommendations, and any items that need office review.

Can I use the same report format for AC, furnace, and refrigeration work?

Yes, the basic structure can be similar, but the details should match the job type. AC repairs, furnace repairs, maintenance visits, and refrigeration service calls may need different operating notes and review items.

Should a service report include pricing?

Only include pricing if it has been confirmed. Do not invent prices, discounts, taxes, deposits, warranty terms, or final totals from rough notes.

Can AI write HVAC service reports?

AI can help turn rough technician notes into cleaner wording, but the final report should be reviewed by a person before it is sent to a customer.
